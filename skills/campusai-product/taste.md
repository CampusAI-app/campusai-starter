# What Jacob looks for

Jacob reviews everything that goes into CampusAI, so learning his eye is the fastest way to make
things that get used. This is built from hundreds of his real reactions to real designs. It is
taste, not law: the fixed lines are in `hard-lines.md`.

## The three that matter most

**No empty space.** Dense and horizontal beats tall and sparse. Three cards across, a 2x2 grid, a
row that scrolls sideways, instead of things stacked down the page with air between them. A clean
screen has less empty space, not more. Inputs and instructions still have to be easy to read.

**Show it, do not describe it.** Everything should be visualized so the student can see it at a
glance. A bar that reads "almost full" before you read the number. A dashboard where each card
answers one question. A calendar that shows the week instead of a sentence about it. If a
paragraph is explaining something, ask what picture would replace it.

**Empty states show what you get.** When a student has nothing yet (no resume, no saved jobs, no
classes), the page shows them what it will look like once they do: a short line at the top, the
main action as a button, and an example built from the real components with sample data, clearly
marked as an example. Never a pop-up or a takeover. The empty page is a preview, not a dead end.

## Layout
- Few boxes. Borders, outlines and dotted lines read as cheap. Separate with space and type.
- A ledger or table beats a stack of cards on a page where the student is working through things.
- Cards that sit side by side share one shape.
- One icon set, the one the dashboard uses.
- Short labels. The explanation lives behind a "?" or a "Why?", not in the label.
- Buttons say the action in plain student words. If it needs explaining, the label is wrong.

## Motion
- Motion announces something: the AI is thinking, a thing was added, a task was checked off.
  Every commit animates (add, save, join, check off) so the student sees where it went.
- Text from the AI streams in like the chat does, never typed out letter by letter.
- One tempo per page. Lively, not faint, but the product itself stays calm.
- Hover grows the thing in place into the real card, never a separate floating card.
- Pop-ups open in the center, from the bottom or the top. Never sliding in from the right.

## Colour and look
- CampusAI is deep crimson, with Maryland gold as the second colour. Gradients go dark red to near
  black, never grey. Not blue.
- Green reads as AI slop in CampusAI. Use the brand red.
- No rainbow of colours for categories. Colour is for status, everything else stays neutral.
- Real everything: the real logo, the real fonts, the real components from the demo. A lookalike
  version is the most common reason something gets rejected. Copy the demo.
- Fonts that scream AI on sight: Space Grotesk, Fraunces, Instrument Serif.
- Glass is fine when it earns it (text stays readable, the blur reads as depth).

## What his words mean
- **"Clean"**: tight, consistent, uncluttered, on brand. Less empty space, not more.
- **"Basic"**: a default is showing (a default font, colour, component or layout). Find which.
- **"Premium"**: consistent and tight on the product. On a website, real motion and real photos.
- **"Elevate"**: a better way to show and move what is already there. Not a new feature.
- His praise goes "decent", "good", "pretty damn good", "really, really like this".

## How he wants to see work
- Options first on anything visual, and he picks.
- One page that holds every option, each one visible and running. Not a list of descriptions.
- Borrowed, not authored. Someone spent hours on the good version already.
