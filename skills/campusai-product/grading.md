# Grading components

Twice a week a routine finds components, animations and techniques from the creators and
libraries we follow, rebuilds them, and puts each one on a card. Jacob grades them, the good ones
get a home in the product. The team grades too, and two opinions are better than one.

**The site:** https://elevations.vercel.app. Sign in with your Base email and password, and your
grades are saved under your name. Every Thursday your Base tasks say "Grade this week's
Elevations". Grading is blind: Jacob's score and the team's only show up after you give yours, so
you are not anchored on anyone.

## What a score means

1 to 10.
- **8 to 10.** I would put this in the product, and I can name where.
- **5 to 7.** Fine, maybe useful, nothing special.
- **1 to 4.** It would make the product worse: noisy, confusing, slow, or it looks generated.

## What to judge

Ask these in order. The first one matters most.

1. **Does it make something clearer for a student?** Motion and effects earn their place by
   explaining something (where a thing came from, what just changed, what to press next). Being
   impressive is not enough.
2. **Is there a real place for it?** Name the page and the spot. "The organizations page, the
   carousel at the top" is a grade. "Could be cool somewhere" is not.
3. **Does it look decided, not generated?** See `hard-lines.md` for what generated looks like.
4. **Would it hold up?** On a phone, with real data instead of perfect sample data, used for the
   twentieth time instead of the first.

## The note

One sentence why, and where it would go. That sentence is what makes a grade useful. Over time
your notes teach the routine what to bring next.
