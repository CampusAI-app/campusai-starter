Getting your thoughts across

This is the most important part. Claude can build almost anything. What it cannot do is guess what you meant. The quality of what comes back depends mostly on how well you said what you wanted, so this is the skill to practise hardest.

Say who and why, not just what

"A freshman with no resume opens Job Match and has no idea where to start" gets a far better result than "make Job Match better". Start with the student and the moment they are in.

Talk, do not type

Say it out loud with Wispr Flow (free). Rambling is fine. Claude reads the intent, and you say more of what you actually think when you are not editing yourself.

Show it

Paste a link, a screenshot, or a component you like. "Like this, but for our Events page" beats a paragraph describing it.

Say what you are reacting to

Not just "I don't like it". Say why: "three things fight for attention, so I don't know where to look first". That is what Claude can fix.

Say how sure you are

"Make this cleaner" is direction, and Claude will come back with options. "Specifically, make the button red" is exact, and it does just that. "Just go" means build it without checking in first.

One thing at a time

One change per ask. Five changes in one message come back as five half-done changes and no way to tell which one broke something.

The three habits

This is the job, and Jacob will teach you each one. Here is what they are, so you know what is coming.

Base

Base is the team's own platform. Jacob sends you an invite by email: open it, set a password, and sign in at base-one-fawn.vercel.app. You will see your Tasks, Updates and Testing. When the team ships something, a test run shows up in Testing with your name on it. Go one feature at a time: Works, Something's off, or Can't test this. Fresh eyes are the most useful thing you have, and they fade in about a week.

Whenever a run arrives

Grade

Twice a week a routine finds new components and puts each on a card at elevations.vercel.app. Score each one 1 to 10, with one sentence on why and where it would go. Jacob's score stays hidden until you give yours.

Every Thursday

Build

Find something you like, pick one place in CampusAI it would make better, and say it in one sentence: "The Save button on Job Match should fill like this." Open Claude in your copy, paste the link and the sentence, and say add a component.

How the iteration process works

Nothing good comes out right the first time. The first version is a draft, and good work usually takes several rounds. This is the loop every piece of work goes through.

Say it

Who it is for, what is wrong for them, and what you want instead. One thing.

See options first

Ask Claude to find two or three ways others already solved it. Borrow before you build.

Pick one

Choose the option, or the best parts of two. That call is yours, not Claude's.

Claude builds it

In your copy of CampusAI, using the real colours, fonts and components.

Look and evaluate

Say what you expect, then press it, on a phone width too. Run the checks below.

Go again, then ship

Say exactly what is off and repeat. When it is right, put it on your link and send Jacob the link and your one sentence.

Evaluating the output

Always be looking, evaluating, iterating. Claude sounds exactly as sure when it is wrong as when it is right, so the skill that matters most is not getting Claude to build something. It is looking at what it built, deciding whether it is actually right, and going again until it is. Every check below is here because skipping it already cost us something real.

The habit that makes it easy. Say what you expect before you look. "When I press Add, the event should show up in Your week." Then press it and compare. Without an expectation, whatever appears looks fine.
1

"Done" is a claim

Open the link and press the thing yourself. Never pass along "it works" that you did not see.

2

Look for what disappeared

A change can quietly remove something. One of ours deleted an onboarding question meant to stay.

3

Saved is not used

A button said Saved while the job feed never moved, because nothing read what it saved. Check what should change.

4

Your link may only work for you

It opens for you because you are logged in. Open it in a private window before sending it.

5

Perfect data hides problems

Try a long name, an empty account, a phone-width window, the back button, pressing twice.

6

Words must match the screen

We have shipped "Roles matching zzzzz" over finance roles. Read every label as the student would.

7

Check any "it says so"

We once called a waitlist live because a code comment said so. It had been deleted two months earlier.

8

Then go again

Finding something off is the job, not a failure. Say exactly what is wrong, let Claude fix it, and look again.

What to ask Claude

  • What did you change, what did you remove, and what did you not check?
  • Show me it at phone width, and in dark mode.
  • What exactly did you click to confirm that works?
  • If this was wrong, how would we know?

How to report it

When you send work to Jacob, say which checks you ran and which you did not. "I checked it on a phone and in a private window, I did not try an empty account" is a good report. "Done" is not.

How the connections work

A connection (people call them MCPs) is a plug that lets Claude use another tool directly. Without one, Claude only knows what it remembers. With one, it can go and look something up, or do something for you, live.

You just ask

You never call a connection yourself. Say what you want in plain words, like "search the component libraries for a button that fills while you hold it" or "put this on my Vercel", and Claude picks the right one.

What yours can do

Search eight component libraries and pull the real code. Look at real websites and Motion's examples. Read up-to-date docs and explain any GitHub repo. Open what it built and check it on a phone width. Deploy to Vercel, use Supabase, and drive Raylight.

Seeing and signing in

Type /mcp in a chat to see every connection and whether it is working. Vercel, Supabase and Raylight ask you to sign in in your browser the first time Claude uses them.

Where to look

Borrow before you build. Someone has almost always made the thing already, and a part used by thousands of people beats one written once in a chat.

Four rules

  • Push only to your own copy of the demo.
  • Deploy only to your own Vercel.
  • Never put a real student's information in anything you build.
  • Nothing goes into the real product, gets posted, or is shown to students before Jacob sees it.

Things to say to Claude after

  • I'm testing the calendar. Help me go through it like a freshman would.
  • Here's my voice memo. Turn it into feedback I can send.
  • Find me three ways other apps do a filter bar, then build the best one in our style.
  • Is this good? Check it against our hard lines.
