2Getting your thoughts across

The most important part. Claude can build almost anything, but it cannot guess what you meant.

Just say it, with Wispr Flow

Wispr Flow (free) turns what you say into clean text. Hold a key and talk it through. Your whole thought process comes across without worrying about every word.

screenshot.png a link a component

Show it

Claude mostly reads code. It cannot see the page the way you do, so show it what you mean.

Say who and why

"A freshman with no resume opens Job Match and does not know where to start" beats "make Job Match better".

Say what you react to

Not "I don't like it", but why: "three things fight for attention".

Say how sure you are

"Make it cleaner" gets options. "Specifically, make it red" gets exactly that.

Start small

Begin with one small change and see how Claude handles it. Once you know, ask for more at once.

3The work, and how it goes

Testing comes first, because it trains the core skill: noticing when something is wrong for a student. Jacob will walk you through Base, where it all lives.

Start by knowing the product

Before you build anything, go through all of CampusAI in the demo. Press everything, try to break it, and learn what each part is for. You cannot make something better that you do not understand.

Testing

Whenever a test run arrives

A run shows up in Base with your name on it. Go one feature at a time: Works, Something's off, or Can't test this, then a 1 to 10. Be a specific student, check what a page shows before anything is saved, try to break it. Fresh eyes fade in about a week.

Grading

Every Thursday

New components land as cards at elevations.vercel.app. Score each 1 to 10, with one sentence on why and where in CampusAI it would go.

Building

Your own time

Pick one thing and one place: "The Save button on Job Match should fill like this." Paste the link and the sentence into Claude in your copy and say add a component.

Every piece of work goes round this loop
1

Say it, then see options

Who it is for and what is wrong. Ask Claude for two or three ways others solved it.

2

Pick, and let Claude build

The pick is yours. Claude builds it in your copy with the real colours and parts.

3

Look, then go again

Say what you expect, press it, say what is off. When it is right, put it on your link and send Jacob.

Into the real product: a pull request

Your work goes into the real CampusAI as a pull request (a PR) on GitHub. Practice the steps on your own copy now, they are the same once you get access. Tell Claude "send this as a PR" and it walks you through them. Jacob reviews every PR first, then the engineering lead. Every PR says three things: how to test each feature, anything you removed, and what the backend would have to do.

4Evaluating the output

Always be looking, evaluating, iterating. Claude sounds just as sure when it is wrong, so say what you expect before you look, then check.

1

"Done" is a claim

Open the link and press the thing yourself.

2

Look for what disappeared

A change can quietly remove something.

3

Saved is not used

Check the thing that should have changed.

4

Your link may only work for you

Open it in a private window first.

5

Perfect data hides problems

Long names, empty accounts, a phone.

6

Words match the screen

Read every label as the student would.

7

Check any "it says so"

Go look at the thing it points to.

8

Then go again

Finding something off is the job.

Ask Claude: "What did you change, what did you remove, and what did you not check?" Report to Jacob which checks you ran and which you did not. "Done" is not a report.

5Skills, connections and components

Claude is the tool. What makes it better is what you connect to it, and the more you give it, the smarter it gets.

Skills

A skill is an exact set of instructions for how to do something: first this, then that, using these parts. People post them on GitHub for everything from motion to graphics. Your setup installs the ones we use, and you can add more by sending Claude a link.

Connections

A connection (an MCP) lets Claude use another tool directly. You never call one. You just ask, and Claude picks the right one. Type /mcp in a chat to see yours.

7Making content

For anyone making CampusAI videos and graphics. The studio is the same one Jacob uses, so every rule and part in it is his, and it stays current on its own.

1

Have four things

Git, Node (the LTS button at nodejs.org), Google Chrome and the Claude app. On Windows, also Git for Windows.

2

Paste the line

Claude installs the studio, its fonts and its skills, and stops when it needs you.

3

Say what you want

"Cut my study spot interviews and end on CampusAI answering." It pitches an idea and a structure first, and builds only once you pick. Or name a style: "/teardown the planner".

4

Send it to Jacob

A Base task called "Video review:" plus what it shows, with the file attached. Nothing gets posted until he says yes there.

Connect Claude to Base once, so Claude can send that task for you. Open Connect Claude (sign in if it asks, it brings you straight back), press the button, paste what it gives you into Claude, then open a new chat.

8Four rules

These never move.

Your own copy only

Push only to your copy of the demo.

Your own Vercel only

Never deploy to anyone else's.

No real student data

Not in a prompt, a file or a build.

Jacob sees it first

Nothing goes into the product, gets posted, or reaches students before he does.
