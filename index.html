The three habits

This is the job. Each one teaches the next.

Test

When the team ships something, a test run shows up on Base's Testing page with your name on it. Go one feature at a time: Works, Something's off, or Can't test this. Anything else goes through the Feedback button in CampusAI. Fresh eyes are the most useful thing you have, and they fade in about a week.

Whenever a run arrives

Grade

Twice a week a routine finds new components and puts each on a card at elevations.vercel.app. Score each one 1 to 10, with one sentence on why and where it would go. Jacob's score stays hidden until you give yours.

Every Thursday

Build

Find something you like, pick one place in CampusAI it would make better, and say it in one sentence: "The Save button on Job Match should fill like this." Open Claude in your copy, paste the link and the sentence, and say add a component.

Your own time

Evaluating the output

Claude sounds exactly as sure when it is wrong as when it is right. So the most valuable skill here is not getting Claude to build something. It is deciding whether what it built is actually true. Every check below is here because skipping it already cost us something real.

The habit that makes it easy. Say what you expect before you look. "When I press Add, the event should show up in Your week." Then press it and compare. Without an expectation, whatever appears looks fine.
1

"Done" is a claim

Open the link and press the thing yourself. Never pass along "it works" that you did not see.

2

Green is not working

We shipped a tour that turned the whole app black with all 2,000 tests passing. Only looking catches that.

3

Look for what disappeared

A change can quietly remove something. One of ours deleted an onboarding question meant to stay.

4

Saved is not used

A button said Saved while the job feed never moved, because nothing read what it saved. Check what should change.

5

Your link may only work for you

It opens for you because you are logged in. Open it in a private window before sending it.

6

Perfect data hides problems

Try a long name, an empty account, a phone-width window, the back button, pressing twice.

7

Words must match the screen

We have shipped "Roles matching zzzzz" over finance roles. Read every label as the student would.

8

Check any "it says so"

We once called a waitlist live because a code comment said so. It had been deleted two months earlier.

What to ask Claude

  • What did you change, what did you remove, and what did you not check?
  • Show me it at phone width, and in dark mode.
  • What exactly did you click to confirm that works?
  • If this was wrong, how would we know?

How to report it

When you send work to Jacob, say which checks you ran and which you did not. "I checked it on a phone and in a private window, I did not try an empty account" is a good report. "Done" is not.

Four rules

  • Push only to your own copy of the demo.
  • Deploy only to your own Vercel.
  • Never put a real student's information in anything you build.
  • Nothing goes into the real product, gets posted, or is shown to students before Jacob sees it.

Things to say to Claude after

  • I'm testing the calendar. Help me go through it like a freshman would.
  • Here's my voice memo. Turn it into feedback I can send.
  • Find me three ways other apps do a filter bar, then build the best one in our style.
  • Is this good? Check it against our hard lines.
